Academic Architecture

A clear route from IFRS knowledge to professional reporting performance.

The SBR class is not arranged as a loose list of accounting standards. It is structured to build the habits required in the examination: understand the principle, identify the reporting issue, explain the treatment clearly and communicate professional judgment.

Exam Objective

Train the student to discuss financial reporting issues with professional judgment.

The course develops competence in IFRS application, ethical reporting behaviour, group accounting and written explanations so students can produce focused answers under time constraints.

Course Discipline

Convert teaching into written performance through assignments, revision and mock practice.

Assignments are positioned throughout the route so students can practise calculations, explanations, application and professional communication before final revision.

Learning Pathway

The outline builds reporting judgment progressively.

Each phase has a clear purpose: establish reporting discipline, build IFRS application capability, then refine final exam performance.

01

Reporting principles and professional ethics

The early phase establishes professional and ethical behaviour, the conceptual framework, presentation, disclosure and revenue recognition.

02

Core IFRS application and scenario judgment

The middle phase develops the major standards, including non-current assets, financial instruments, leases, employee benefits, income taxes, provisions, share-based payment and fair value measurement.

03

Groups, foreign currency and examination execution

The final teaching phase builds group accounting, statements of cash flows, foreign transactions and entities, followed by structured question revision and mock preparation.

Execution Model

SBR success depends on principle, application and professional explanation.

The learning model is designed to move students away from memorised accounting rules and train them to explain financial reporting treatment in the language of the exam.

01

Principle Foundation

Understand the reporting principle before attempting calculations or written discussion.

02

Scenario Reading

Identify the transaction, reporting issue and required treatment before drafting.

03

Technical Application

Apply IFRS requirements through structured calculations, journal logic and explanations.

04

Feedback Cycle

Use assignment and revision work to correct weak presentation before mock practice.

Academic Phases

The SBR route is spacious, readable and easy to follow.

The class journey is organised into three broad phases so students can see where each topic fits within the bigger examination strategy.

01
Foundation Phase

Establish the professional and conceptual base of the SBR paper.

This phase covers ethical behaviour, the conceptual framework, presentation and disclosure, and revenue recognition so students begin with the language of reporting judgment.

  • Ethical reporting behaviour
  • Conceptual framework
  • Presentation and revenue
02
Application Phase

Build competence in the major IFRS standards tested in SBR.

This phase focuses on assets, financial instruments, leases, employee benefits, tax, provisions, share-based payment, fair value and other reporting issues.

  • Standards application
  • Calculation discipline
  • Professional explanation
03
Revision Phase

Convert reporting knowledge into final exam discipline.

The revision phase uses structured question practice across SBR Questions 1 to 4, followed by a mock exam to strengthen timing, relevance and presentation.

  • Questions 1 and 2 practice
  • Questions 3 and 4 practice
  • Mock exam checkpoint
